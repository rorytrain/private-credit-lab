Hmm. "Temperature zero makes the model predictable but does not make it correct — if the prompt is ambiguous, it will predictably misinterpret it every time." Surely that is both prompt and a temperature adjustment and refinement.

Temperature controls variance. Prompt controls direction. You need both levers and they are not substitutes for each other. A well-constructed prompt at temperature 0.7 will outperform an ambiguous prompt at temperature zero every time — because variance around the wrong target is still wrong. The optimal configuration is a precise prompt at low temperature: you are pointing the model accurately and then removing the wobble.
The scoring inconsistency between Version A and Version B illustrates exactly that. Temperature zero locked in a number but the prompt did not give the model enough discriminating instruction to differentiate a dividend recap from a growth capital deployment at the scoring level. The direction was slightly off and the model reproduced that error reliably. That is your Day 6 refinement — tighten the mandate fit scoring rubric to explicitly weight use of proceeds as a scoring variable, not just a flag.
